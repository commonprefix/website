Writing a post

Guidelines for the team · updated on November 24, 2026 · not listed on the Blog

This page is for the team: it is not on the Blog list and search engines do not index it. It shows how a post is put together on the new site and how every part of one looks, so a new post can be checked against it.

Making a post

  1. In common-prefix-coder, copy a post in blog/ to blog/<slug>.html. The slug is the address: lowercase words joined by hyphens, as in sei-giga-consensus.
  2. Change the <title>, the description, the title (.post-title), the byline (.post-meta) and the text (.post-body). Leave the top bar, the seal and the footer as they are.
  3. Add the post to the top of the list in blog.html, with the same date and authors as its byline.
  4. Build, copy the build into the website repository and open a pull request (its README has the steps). Check the post on a phone in the Vercel preview before merging.

The byline

Under the title, in one line: the date, written out (on November 24, 2026), the authors, and the reading time at about 200 words a minute. A member of the team is a link to their Team page with data-person, so the name opens their sheet in place:

<p class="post-meta">on November 24, 2026 by <a href="/team#dionysis" data-person="dionysis">Dionysis Zindros</a> · 4 min read</p>

A co-author from outside the team is plain text, without a link.

Text

Write in paragraphs of a few sentences. The text is set in Computer Modern and justified, like a paper; on a phone it is ragged on the right. Use bold for the one phrase a reader must not miss, italics for terms and titles, and code for names from code. Links are in pink and open in place when they stay on the site.

Section titles

Use h2 for sections and h3 for the parts of a section, rarely h4. Never h1: the post's title is the page's only one. Titles are short and say what the section shows, not "Introduction".

Quotes

A protocol is only as secure as the assumptions it is proven under.

A quote is for someone else's words. For an aside of your own, a paragraph is better.

Lists

  • Bullets for things that have no order.
  • Each item a full sentence, or each one a short phrase, but not both.
    • A second level when an item has parts.
  1. Numbers for steps, or for a ranking.
  2. The reader can then refer to "step 2".

Code

A block of code names its language in the class, and is coloured once the page has loaded (Prism):

fn quorum(n: usize) -> usize {
    // More than two thirds of the validators.
    2 * n / 3 + 1
}

Languages already used in posts: bash, cpp, rust, solidity, ts and html. A block without a language stays plain.

Formulas

Formulas are typeset by MathJax, which loads only on posts that have one. Inline, inside a sentence: \(f(x, y) = \sum_i a_i \sin(k_i \cdot (x \cos\theta_i + y \sin\theta_i) + \varphi_i)\), the equation behind the lines at the foot of every page. On a line of its own:

\[\forall P_1, P_2, r_1, r_2 : r_1 \le r_2 \Rightarrow C^{P_1}_{r_1}[:-k] \preceq C^{P_2}_{r_2}\]

Write them as <span class="math inline">\( … \)</span> and <span class="math display">\[ … \]</span>.

A wide table scrolls sideways inside the column instead of widening the page.

Images

Images go in the website repository under public/static/blog/<slug>/, at most twice the width they are shown at (about 1440px), as JPEG for photographs and SVG or PNG for diagrams. Every image has an alt that says what it shows.

Footnotes

Sources go in footnotes, not in the sentence.1 A footnote can also hold an aside that would interrupt the text.2 They are numbered in order and listed at the end of the post.
